# THE HOUSE ON MERCER LANE
### A PSX-Style Psychological Horror Game — Full Story & Narrative Document
### Version 2.0 — Revised Against Critical Assessment

---

## CORE CONCEPT (ONE SENTENCE)

A six-year-old boy named Leo replays the last night of his life, believing he's waiting for his mother to come home — until the house itself forces him to understand that she already did.

---

## DIALOGUE FORMAT NOTE

This is a **dialogue-based game**. All character communication is delivered as on-screen text in PSX-style dialogue boxes — no voice acting. Characters speak through:
- **Memory fragment dialogue boxes** — triggered by interacting with objects; render as past-tense conversations Leo witnessed or participated in
- **Leo's internal monologue** — rendered as smaller, italicised text in a separate box style, always in present tense
- **Environmental text** — notes, labels, a child's drawings with words on them

Dialogue boxes appear with a small portrait icon indicating the speaker. Ray's portrait is always slightly turned away. Sandra's portrait is always slightly downcast. Leo's portrait is always looking directly forward.

---

## NARRATIVE PREMISE

Leo is dead.

He died on a Tuesday night in November, at the bottom of the basement stairs, from a head injury sustained when his father struck him during a drunken rage — and from the internal bleeding that followed across four hours of silence while his father slept it off upstairs and his mother worked a double shift at the diner.

Leo doesn't know this.

The game begins with Leo waking up on the living room couch, the TV on static, the house dark except for the kitchen light. He thinks he fell asleep waiting for Mom. He thinks it's just another night. He thinks if he's quiet enough, small enough, invisible enough — everything will be okay.

It won't be. It never was.

**The horror of this game is not what happens. It's what already happened.**

---

## NARRATIVE ARCHITECTURE

The game is structured as a **memory loop** — Leo's consciousness replaying the last night of his life in fragments, each pass through the house revealing more of the truth. The house is not haunted. Leo is the haunting.

There are **three loops**, each distinctly different in tone, available interactions, and emotional weight:

- **Loop One:** Leo believes it's a normal night. Simple interactions. The house is slightly too quiet. Memory fragments are warm and ordinary — designed to establish characters before they're broken.
- **Loop Two:** The house resists. Memory fragments are fractured, contradictory. Ray's presence becomes felt rather than inferred. New interactions unlock. Leo starts asking questions he can't finish.
- **Loop Three:** The loop collapses. The house shows what it's been hiding. The basement door opens. New rooms within known rooms. The truth arrives in pieces and then all at once.

**Each loop is mechanically distinct** — new objects become interactive, existing objects return different dialogue, the house layout develops subtle wrongnesses that compound across loops.

## CHARACTERS

### LEO (Protagonist, 6 years old)
Leo is small. The kind of small where doorknobs are at eye level and the kitchen counter is a horizon. He has brown eyes, a gap where his two front teeth used to be, and he always wears the same red-and-white striped shirt — the one his mother bought him on his last birthday at the discount store on Fifth.

Leo is not stupid. He is six. There is a difference. He has learned, over years of a shortened life, to read rooms — to understand the temperature of a house by the way a door closes, by whether the TV is too loud, by what his father's breathing sounds like from across the hallway. Leo is an expert in invisible things.

**Leo's internal monologue is the game's primary voice.** He narrates what he sees, feels, and almost-understands in the present tense. His observations are a child's — literal, precise about the wrong things, missing the implications the player can see. This gap between what Leo says and what the player understands is where the horror lives.

**Leo's dialogue with memory-characters** is delivered in past-tense fragments. When a memory plays, Leo is both inside it (participating as his past self) and watching it (as his present dead self). The dialogue boxes distinguish these states through text style.

---

### THE FATHER — RAYMOND (Ray)
Raymond Aldric is 34 years old and looks 50.

Ray was laid off from the sheet metal factory fourteen months before the game's events. He has not looked for work in nine. He drinks to cope with not working. He doesn't work because he drinks. He has convinced himself both facts are caused by external forces.

**Ray is not a monster in the cinematic sense.** He is a man who has outsourced the labor of facing himself so completely that when he raises his hand, he genuinely believes something drove him to it. This is more frightening than deliberate cruelty. Deliberate cruelty has a shape you can see coming. Ray is formless. Ray is weather.

**Ray's presence in the game is rebuilt for Version 2** to address the underwriting criticism. He is now felt in three dimensions:

**1. Physical evidence (existing, unchanged)**
Empty bottles. A broken chair leg. A hole in the drywall at adult-eye-level.

**2. Dialogue fragments — Ray's own words, retrieved through objects**
Every object associated with Ray carries a memory dialogue fragment. These fragments deliberately show BOTH sides of Ray — the version that made Sandra marry him and the version that killed his son. The horror is that both are real and neither cancels the other.

**3. The Upstairs Sequence (new in Loop Two)**
A new interactive sequence: Leo hears Ray stirring upstairs. The player must navigate the upstairs hallway while Ray moves around in his bedroom. Ray never exits the bedroom — but his proximity is felt through sound, through the light under the door, through his voice (via dialogue boxes) talking to himself. Leo must retrieve something from his room without waking Ray. This sequence has no fail state — but the player won't know that. The tension is the point.

**Ray's complete dialogue profile:**

*Memory Fragment: "The Good Version" (triggered by old family photo, Loop One)*
> RAY: "Come here, bud. You're gonna watch the game with me."
> LEO: "I don't know the rules."
> RAY: "Neither do I. We'll make some up."
> [Leo's internal monologue]: *He smelled like soap that night. Not the other smell.*

*Memory Fragment: "The Promise" (triggered by Ray's work boots by the door, Loop Two)*
> RAY: "I'm gonna find something. Welding, maybe. I still know people at the yard."
> SANDRA: "Ray."
> RAY: "I mean it this time. I talked to Marcus."
> SANDRA: "Okay."
> RAY: "You don't believe me."
> SANDRA: "I believe you, Ray."
> [Leo's internal monologue]: *She said it the way you say something when you're very tired.*

*Memory Fragment: "The Argument" (triggered by broken chair, Loop Two)*
> [Audio only — no portraits. Just text on black.]
> RAY: "I'm in my own house. I'll do what I want in my own house."
> SANDRA: [quiet] "Leo's right upstairs."
> RAY: "He's asleep."
> SANDRA: "Ray—"
> [Sound: something heavy hitting the floor.]
> [Silence.]
> RAY: [quieter] "I didn't mean—"
> [Sound: footsteps leaving the room.]

*Memory Fragment: "Small Moment" (triggered by Leo's drawing of Ray, Loop One)*
> RAY: "Is that supposed to be me?"
> LEO: "You have yellow hair in it because I only had yellow."
> RAY: "I don't have yellow hair."
> LEO: "I know. But you have a smile."
> [Ray says nothing. Then:]
> RAY: "Yeah. Yeah, I do."
> [Leo's internal monologue]: *He kept it. I saw him put it in his pocket.*

*Memory Fragment: "The Night" (triggered by basement door, Loop Three — partial)*
> [Text renders slower than usual. Each line takes longer to appear.]
> RAY: "—told you not to—"
> LEO: "I was just—"
> RAY: "—don't—"
> [Dialogue box glitches. Text corrupts. Clears.]
> [Leo's internal monologue]: *I don't remember after the loud part.*

### THE MOTHER — SANDRA
Sandra Aldric is 31. She works a double shift most nights at the Sunrise Diner on Route 9. She smells like coffee and dish soap and, faintly, something floral. She has a laugh that comes from somewhere real when it comes at all.

**Sandra's guilt is now fully explored** through a new set of memory fragments that address her choices, her reasons for staying, and the specific texture of her love for Leo. The player is given enough to feel complicated sympathy — not full sympathy, not full condemnation. Something in between. That in-between is the point.

**Sandra's complete dialogue profile:**

*Memory Fragment: "Why She Stays" (triggered by wedding photo, Loop Two)*
> SANDRA: [on the phone, Leo listening from the hallway]
> SANDRA: "He's not always like this, Mom."
> [Pause.]
> SANDRA: "I know what you said. But Leo needs—"
> [Pause.]
> SANDRA: "I'm not going to — I can't just—"
> [Pause — longer this time.]
> SANDRA: "I know, Mom. I know."
> [She doesn't say anything for a while after that.]
> [Leo's internal monologue]: *I went back to my room. I didn't want her to know I heard.*

*Memory Fragment: "The Apology She Couldn't Make" (triggered by Leo's cereal bowl, Loop Two)*
> SANDRA: "I'm sorry I missed dinner again."
> LEO: "It's okay."
> SANDRA: "It's not, baby. I know it's not."
> LEO: "You left the good cereal."
> SANDRA: [laughs — the real one] "I left the good cereal."
> LEO: "So it's okay."
> [Leo's internal monologue]: *She hugged me for a long time after that. I let her.*

*Memory Fragment: "What She Planned" (triggered by her coat on the hook, Loop Three)*
> [This is not a dialogue. It's a note, handwritten, found in Sandra's coat pocket.]
> [The note reads:]
> "Call Donna at the shelter Monday. Ask about the two-bedroom. Ask if they take kids. Don't tell Ray. Don't tell anyone yet. Just ask."
> [The note is dated six days before the game's events.]
> [Leo's internal monologue]: *I don't know what this means. But it smells like her.*

*Memory Fragment: "The Last Morning" (triggered by the note on the fridge, Loop Three)*
> SANDRA: "Leo. Leo, wake up."
> LEO: [groggy] "Mm."
> SANDRA: "I'm going to work. I left food."
> LEO: "The note says it."
> SANDRA: "You can read it now?"
> LEO: "Most of it. 'Love Mom' is easy."
> [Sandra says nothing for a moment.]
> SANDRA: "Yeah. That part's easy."
> [She kisses him on the forehead.]
> [Leo's internal monologue]: *That was this morning. It feels like a long time ago now.*

## THE HOUSE — MERCER LANE

The house at Mercer Lane is a two-story rental with bad insulation, a leaking kitchen tap, carpet that remembers every stain, and a basement that nobody uses except to store what the family can't afford to replace and isn't ready to throw away.

**The house has five navigable areas** (basement added as a partial area in Loop Two, full area in Loop Three):

---

### 1. THE LIVING ROOM

**Loop One interactions (8 objects):**
- Blanket → Leo's internal monologue: *"It smells like her. The good smell, not the work smell."*
- TV (static) → Leo tries every channel. Settles on the static. *"Maybe it'll fix itself."*
- Family photo → Triggers "Small Moment" dialogue with Ray (Leo's drawing)
- VCR clock (11:47 PM) → *"That can't be right. It's been longer than that."*
- Couch cushion → finds the empty Jameson bottle. *"I'm not supposed to touch those."* Puts it back.
- Window → Leo looks outside. Street is empty. *"She usually turns the headlights off before she pulls in. So you can see them coming from the window."*
- Loose floorboard (near hallway) → Leo steps on it deliberately. *"Thump. I like that sound."*
- Carpet stain (near TV) → *"Dad said it was juice. Mom didn't say anything."*

**Loop Two additions:**
- Empty bottle now has the "Argument" memory fragment attached
- Family photo now shows Ray's face slightly wrong — Leo notices: *"Dad's face looks like when the TV goes blurry."*
- Loose floorboard returns a different response: *"I don't want to make noise. Not tonight."*

**Loop Three:**
- Family photo is just Leo. He stares at it.
- *"That's me. But I don't remember when we took this one."*
- *"I don't think we took this one."*

---

### 2. THE KITCHEN

The most "alive" room in the game. The dripping tap is the room's heartbeat.

**Loop One interactions (9 objects):**
- Note on fridge → Leo reads it aloud, slowly: *"Left... dinner... in the pot. Be good. Love, Mom."* Then: *"I already know how to be good."*
- Pot on stove → still warm. *"She made the soup with the little noodles. I like those ones."*
- Cereal bowl on counter → half-eaten. *"I was going to finish it. I just got tired."*
- Fridge (open) → Leo looks inside. *"There's the good juice. I'm not allowed to have it at night."* Closes fridge.
- Tap (dripping) → Leo tries to tighten it. Can't reach properly. *"It always does that. Dad says he'll fix it."*
- Calendar on wall → October. Several dates crossed out. One circled: "RENT." Leo: *"That's the bad day. The day they talk loud."*
- Dish rack → drying. *"She washed up before she left. She always does that."*
- Medicine bottles on counter → Loop One: Leo doesn't interact. They're just there. (Loop Two, they become interactive.)
- Back door → locked. *"Locked from outside. That's wrong."*

**Loop Two additions:**
- Medicine bottles → *"These are Mom's. For headaches, she says."* Internal monologue underneath: *"She gets a lot of headaches."*
- Note on fridge triggers "The Apology She Couldn't Make" fragment
- Cereal bowl is now empty. Leo stares at it. *"I don't remember finishing it."*
- Tap is louder. Leo can't make it stop.

**Loop Three:**
- "What She Planned" note is found not on the fridge but in Sandra's coat in the kitchen — fell out. Leo picks it up. He can't read all of it.
- Pot is cold. *"It's been a long time. The soup's gone cold."*
- Tap is synced to a second rhythm now — something underneath it. The player will notice before Leo does.

---

### 3. THE UPSTAIRS HALLWAY & ROOMS

**Ray's Bedroom Door**

The door is always closed. It never opens. But it is never inert.

Loop One: Leo passes it fast. Internal monologue: *"He's asleep. The snoring means asleep."*
Loop Two: The door is where the Upstairs Sequence takes place (see below).
Loop Three: The snoring has stopped. Silence from behind the door. Leo doesn't remark on this directly. The player will.

**The Upstairs Sequence (Loop Two — new interactive section)**

Leo needs to get Reg (the stuffed rabbit) from his room. He left him downstairs in Loop One. Going upstairs means passing Ray's door.

The sequence works as follows: Ray is not a threat. But Leo doesn't know that. The player doesn't know that. The tension is constructed entirely from sound and proximity.

Ray's dialogue leaks through the door — not to Leo, just audible — in fragmented dialogue boxes marked [THROUGH DOOR]:

> [THROUGH DOOR] RAY: "...not my fault..."
> [THROUGH DOOR] RAY: "...should've just..."
> [THROUGH DOOR] RAY: [long silence] "...Leo..."

When Ray says "Leo" through the door, the player freezes. Nothing happens. Nothing was going to happen. But the player held their breath, and that is the game working.

Leo reaches his room. Gets Reg. Returns downstairs without incident.

**Leo's Bedroom**

One of the most object-dense rooms in the game. Every loop adds interactions.

*Loop One (6 objects):*
- Reg (rabbit) → *"Reg always waits. That's his job."* Leo picks him up, sets him down again.
- Drawing of family → triggers "Small Moment" Ray memory
- Window → Leo watches the street from his room. *"You can see further from up here."*
- Box under bed → Loop One, Leo doesn't open it. Just looks at it. *"My important stuff."*
- Poster on wall (rocket ship) → *"I want to be an astronaut. Or a dog trainer. I haven't decided."*
- Piggy bank on shelf → *"I have four dollars and some pennies. I'm saving for a game."*

*Loop Two (new interactions):*
- Box under bed → Leo opens it. Sees the contents but doesn't retrieve them yet. *"I keep the things that matter in here. So I know where they are."*
- Drawing of family → Ray's figure is outlined but uncolored. Leo: *"I was going to finish it. I kept forgetting to."*
- Window → a car slows outside. Leo watches it. It doesn't stop. *"That wasn't her car."*

*Loop Three (full box interaction):*
Leo goes through the box object by object. Each item triggers a small dialogue memory:
- Marble → *"Marcus gave me this at school. He said it was special because it had a star inside. I looked for a long time. I think I see it."*
- Button → *"From Mom's good coat. She said she'd sew it back. I saved it so she wouldn't have to look for it."*
- Shell → *"The beach. We went once. Dad carried me on his shoulders in the water and I wasn't scared."* [internal]: *"I want to remember the shoulder part."*
- Folded drawing → unfolded: Leo as an astronaut, Sandra as the ground crew, Ray as a figure waving from far away. Leo: *"I drew everyone in it."* [internal]: *"Everyone."*
- The note "My Brave Boy" → triggers final bedroom memory:

> SANDRA: "Leo. Come here."
> LEO: "I'm not crying."
> SANDRA: "I know you're not."
> LEO: "I'm not."
> SANDRA: "Brave boys still get scared. That's the whole point of being brave."
> LEO: "...Is Dad brave?"
> [Sandra doesn't answer right away.]
> SANDRA: "You are. That's what I know."
> [She wrote the note and handed it to him.]
> SANDRA: "For when I'm not here."
> [Leo's internal monologue, present tense]: *She meant at work. She meant for when she's at work.*
> [Internal monologue, quieter]: *Didn't she?*

---

### 4. THE BATHROOM

Small room. Three interactions across loops.

Loop One: Cracked tile. *"Don't step on that one. I remember."*
Loop Two: Medicine cabinet opens. Empty prescription bottles — Sandra's name, old dates. *"She used to take these. She stopped."* [internal]: *"She said they made her feel far away."*
Loop Three: The mirror. Leo looks in it for the first time. He sees himself — but the reflection is slightly delayed. Leo looks at his hands. Looks at the mirror. *"There's something wrong with the mirror."* He doesn't say what. The player sees it.

---

### 5. THE BASEMENT

**Loop One:** The door. Just a door. Leo's internal monologue if approached: *"I don't go down there."* No interaction available.

**Loop Two:** Door becomes interactive. Cold. Leo touches the handle and pulls back.
> [LEO, internal]: *There's something—*
> [Dialogue box glitches. Cuts.]*
> [Leo steps back.] *"Not tonight."*

**Loop Three — The Basement Sequence (fully revised)**

The door opens on its own. Not dramatically — it just... drifts open. Leo stands in the hallway looking at it for a long moment.

> [LEO, internal]: *I've never seen it do that.*

The descent is interactive — Leo can stop on each stair. Each stair has a dialogue trigger if the player pauses:

Stair 1: *"It smells old down here."*
Stair 2: *"Dad keeps his fishing stuff here. He doesn't fish anymore."*
Stair 3: *"Mom's old boxes from before they moved here."*
Stair 4: *"It's cold."*
Stair 5: *"It's very cold."*
Stair 6: *"I don't—"* [cuts off]
Stair 7: [no dialogue. Leo stops for a long moment. The player can choose to keep going or stay. Either way, eventually, the game moves forward.]

At the bottom: Leo jumps to pull the string. The light comes on.

**The Interactive Reveal:**

Leo sees himself.

This is not a passive cutscene. The player retains control.

They can move Leo closer to his own body. Each step triggers a dialogue:

Step 1: *"That's..."*
Step 2: *"That's my shirt."*
Step 3: *"That's—"*
[Player reaches the body. Leo crouches — automatically — and looks at his own face.]
[Long silence. Dialogue box appears empty. Then, slowly, one word at a time:]

> *"Oh."*

[Another pause.]

> *"Oh. I see."*

[Another pause. Leo sits down next to his own body. The player can now look around the basement from this seated position. Several objects become interactive — as if the game is giving the player time to breathe before it continues:]

- Ray's fishing box → *"He bought this when I was a baby. He said we'd go someday."*
- Sandra's moving box (labeled "BEFORE") → Leo touches the lid. *"Before what?"*
- A child's shoe, single, under the stairs → *"That's mine from when I was little. Mom kept it."*
- The stairs Leo just descended → Leo looks at them. *"I fell."* [internal, slower]: *"I fell and it was very loud and then it was quiet."* [internal, quietest]: *"And then it was just quiet."*

[When the player stands Leo up again, the final monologue begins:]

> *"I was waiting for her."*
> *"I kept thinking she'd come home and everything would be okay."*
> *"That's what happens. She comes home and it's okay again."*
> *"I was very cold."*
> *"I called for Dad."*
> *"He didn't—"*
> [Pause.]
> *"He was asleep."*
> *"I was waiting for a long time."*
> *"She came home."*

## THE ENDING — REVISED (Visual + Audio)

The screen doesn't cut to black immediately. Instead:

**The house, first person, Leo's perspective — but the loop has broken.**

The environment changes in real time. The color palette completes its shift to monochrome. Objects that were interactive become still. The TV static cuts — and for the first time in the game, there is a beat of absolute silence.

Then: **Sandra's key in the lock.**

The front door opens. The player can see — from the basement stairs — the rectangle of light from the living room, at the top of the stairs, visible through the open basement door.

**Sandra's footsteps.** Tired. Twelve hours.

Then: a pause in the footsteps. She sees the open basement door.

> [SANDRA, called from upstairs — dialogue box appears, no portrait]:
> "Leo?"

[Silence.]

> "Leo, why is the—"

[Her footsteps, faster now, toward the basement door. The light from the living room gets brighter as she gets closer.]

[From Leo, very quiet — the quietest dialogue box in the game — no portrait, small text, slightly transparent:]

> *"Mom."*

[Sandra appears at the top of the basement stairs. The player sees her silhouette — backlit, face in shadow. She looks down.]

[The dialogue box for what she says next is empty. It appears. It stays empty. Then it disappears.]

[She descends the stairs slowly. She reaches the bottom. She kneels next to Leo's body.]

[From somewhere that is not quite the basement and not quite anywhere:]

> *"She found me."*
> *"I knew she would."*
> *"She always checks on me. Even when I'm asleep. She always checks."*

[Sandra's silhouette, crouched, not moving.]

[The light from the living room, at the top of the stairs, still on.]

[The last dialogue box:]

> *"It's okay now."*
> *"She's here."*

[Cut to black. The TV static — absent since Sandra arrived — does not return.]

**END.**

## EMOTIONAL ARCHITECTURE — THE UNRESOLVED TENSION

This game does not resolve. It does not offer catharsis. It offers understanding.

The ending is devastating precisely because Leo is comforted. He believes that Sandra arriving means it's okay. The player knows it isn't. The gap between those two things — Leo's peace and the player's understanding of what Sandra is now facing, what Ray is still asleep through, what cannot be undone — is where the game lives after the screen goes black.

**The horror is in the comfort.** Leo dies believing he was loved and found and that everything will be okay now. He was loved. He was found. Everything will not be okay.

That is the whole game.

## REVISION LOG — WEAKNESSES ADDRESSED

| Weakness | Score | Resolution |
|----------|-------|-----------|
| Limited Interaction Variety | 6/10 | 8 interactables in Loop One rising to 15+ by Loop Three. Each loop unlocks new objects and returns new dialogue from existing objects. |
| Loop One Pacing Too Slow | 6/10 | Interaction density increased. Loose floorboard, back door, calendar, cereal bowl — small moments that build character without requiring narrative payoff. Loop One now ends on the first anomaly (family photo) landing harder because the player has spent time with it. |
| Father Underwritten | 6/10 | Ray now has 5 full dialogue fragments spanning both sides of his character. Upstairs Sequence creates embodied fear of Ray's presence without making him a cartoon. The contrast between "The Good Version" and "The Argument" is the horror. |
| Basement Reveal Lacks Interaction | 5/10 | Fully interactive. Seven stairs with individual triggers. Leo walks to his own body in player-controlled steps. Object interactions in the basement after the reveal. The monologue is paced by player movement. |
| Sandra's Guilt Underexplored | 7/10 | Four full Sandra dialogue fragments. "Why She Stays" addresses her inability to leave. "The Note" reveals she was planning to. The gap between that note and what happened is its own horror. |
| Ending Audio-Only | 6/10 | Ending is now visual — Sandra descends the stairs in silhouette, the player sees her find Leo. The audio is still primary but the visual is present. Leo's final comfort is rendered, not just heard. |
| Horror All Emotional | 7/10 | Upstairs Sequence adds genuine tension — no jump scare, but real breath-held dread. Loop Three mirror moment. The basement stairs descent. The body reveal as interactive. Tension without monsters. |
| Emotional Climax Too Quiet | 7/10 | The reveal is now interactive and paced by player movement. The object interactions in the basement after give the player space to absorb before the final monologue. Sandra's arrival and descent give the moment visual weight. |
| Loops Too Similar | 6/10 | Each loop has mechanical distinctions: new objects, changed dialogue, different responses from existing objects. Loop Two adds Upstairs Sequence. Loop Three opens the basement, changes the mirror, empties the cereal bowl, adds Sandra's coat note. The house develops wrongness progressively. |
| "I Am Dead" Revelation Too Guessable | 7/10 | The mirror moment in Loop Three (delayed reflection) introduces a visual hint before the basement, giving players who guess early a moment of dread confirmation rather than a flat reveal. Players who don't guess are still served by the interactive descent and the body reveal. |

## AUDIO DESIGN NOTES

- **The tap dripping:** Rhythm shifts per loop. By Loop Three it syncs with a second sound — barely audible — that the player will eventually identify as a heartbeat. It's Leo's. It stopped hours ago. It's still in the loop.
- **Ray's sleeping:** Loop One and Two: deep, regular, audible from upstairs. Loop Three: absent. The absence is louder than the sound was.
- **Sandra's arrival:** Anticipated across all three loops via sounds that are almost-her (cars, fridge motor, settling house). The real arrival is unmistakable because it's different from all the false ones.
- **Leo's monologue:** No voice acting. Text only. The silence where a child's voice should be is its own horror.
- **The static:** Present from frame one. Cuts when Sandra arrives. Does not return.
- **The Upstairs Sequence:** Ray's voice through the door is text only — dialogue boxes, no audio. This makes it more disorienting, not less. The player reads Ray's words in silence. That silence is the threat.

## VISUAL LANGUAGE

PSX aesthetic: low-poly geometry, limited texture resolution, CRT filter with scanlines and chromatic aberration. The low fidelity serves the horror — it creates distance that makes the emotional content survivable while making the story feel like a memory retrieved from a damaged medium.

**Color palette:**
- Loop One: warm but muted. Amber, soft grey, kitchen-light yellow. The house trying to look like home.
- Loop Two: the warmth starts pulling back. Cooler tones bleed in. The amber becomes jaundiced.
- Loop Three: near-monochrome except for two anchors — Leo's red-and-white striped shirt (always the brightest object in frame, always) and the green of the VCR clock. When Sandra descends the basement stairs, a third color returns briefly: the warm amber of the living room light behind her. It's the only warmth left. It's coming from the wrong direction.

**The camera:** First-person, child-height. The world is huge. The player will feel it in every room.

**Dialogue boxes:** PSX-style bordered text boxes. Speaker name + small portrait icon. Ray's portrait: face slightly turned. Sandra's portrait: eyes slightly downcast. Leo's internal monologue: no portrait, smaller box, italic text, slightly transparent — as if it's inside the frame rather than on top of it.

## THEMATIC STATEMENT

*The House on Mercer Lane* is a game about the specific horror of ordinary love — love that is real and insufficient, love that exists inside structures that destroy the people inside them. It is not a game about evil. It is a game about the gap between intention and impact, about the way damage moves through families like water through walls: slowly, invisibly, until one day the structure fails and everyone is surprised.

Leo loved his parents. Leo died anyway.

That sentence is the whole game.
